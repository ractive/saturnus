The HP 39G is a graphing calculator for school maths that Hewlett-Packard brought out in 2000 to follow the HP 38G. It keeps the 38G's aplets and its screen, but runs on the hardware design of the HP 49G with eight times the memory, and adds aplets for statistical tests and for exploring graphs.

![An HP 39G in Saturnus showing 3 squared](../img/39g-640.webp)
The 39G as Saturnus draws it, with 3² in the HOME view.

Saturnus is a free emulator of the HP 39G. It runs the 39G's own ROM in your browser, as a desktop app for Mac, Windows and Linux, and on the command line.

## History

### After the 38G

The [HP 38G](38g.md) of 1995 had shown what HP wanted in a school calculator: aplets, small applications for one topic each, that a teacher can beam to a whole class. Its successor kept that idea and the same screen, and [HP's user's guide for it lists](https://literature.hpcalc.org/official/hp39g40g-ug-en.pdf) what changed: a memory manager, a way to jump to a value on a plot, tracing along a regression curve, and three new aplets.

The bigger change was inside. The 38G had used the hardware of the 48G. The 39G is built on the newer [HP 49G](49g.md). [Christoph Gießelink](https://hp.giesselink.com/25ann48.htm), who maintains the [Emu48](https://hp.giesselink.com/emu48.htm) emulator, writes that HP's calculator team wanted the new models to stay compatible with the 49G, which made the software easier to develop. The final hardware has 1 MB of ROM and 256 KB of RAM, where the 49G has 2 MB of flash memory and 512 KB of RAM. That is still eight times the 38G's 32 KB of RAM.

### One design, two calculators

HP made two calculators from this design, both in 2000. The 39G, sold in the US, has the infrared port and no computer algebra. The [HP 40G](40g.md), sold in Europe and some other markets, has the 49G's computer algebra system and no infrared. Both run the same ROM. [Wikipedia notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_39/40_series) that the operating system finds out at start-up which model it runs on, and turns the features on or off to match.

[Finseth's data sheet gives](https://www.finseth.com/hpdata/hp39g.php) both models the code name Enterprise and a launch price of $79.99.

### The end and what came after

By January 2003 the price was $69.99, and the 39G was discontinued on 30 May 2003. In September 2003 HP released the 39g+, with a 75 MHz [ARM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture_family) processor, USB and [IrDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IrDA). The 39gs followed in 2006 and the 39gII in 2011. [Wikipedia says](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_39/40_series) the planned successor of the 39gII, with computer algebra, was dropped when its processor was discontinued, and the idea led to the [HP Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_Prime) of 2013.

## What makes it special

### Aplets

[The 39G's user's guide calls](https://literature.hpcalc.org/official/hp39g40g-ug-en.pdf) aplets "e-lessons" too. Each one explores one kind of problem, in three views: PLOT for the graph, SYMB for the equations and NUM for a table of numbers. The APLET key opens the library. These are built in:

- Function, Parametric, Polar and Sequence, for curves and sequences
- Solve, for equations in one or more variables
- Statistics, for one- and two-variable data
- Inference, new on the 39G
- Quad Explorer and Trig Explorer, two teaching aplets, also new

You save a changed aplet under its own name, with its equations, settings, notes and sketches. Many more aplets came from HP's web site and from teachers' sites, often with worksheets for students.

### Inference

The Inference aplet adds statistical tests to the Statistics aplet. From one or two samples it finds confidence intervals and tests hypotheses for a mean, a proportion, or the difference between two means or two proportions, with the normal or [Student's t distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student%27s_t-distribution). Each test's input form starts with example data that gives a meaningful result, so a student can see what the test does before entering their own.

### Explorers

The two teaching aplets show how the numbers in an equation shape its graph. Quad Explorer works with y = a(x + h)² + v, Trig Explorer with y = a sin(bx + c) + d. In one mode you change the equation and watch the graph move; in the other you move the graph with the arrow keys and watch the equation change. The original graph stays on the screen to compare with.

### Sharing in class

Two 39Gs exchange aplets, lists, matrices, notes and programs by infrared: line up the triangle marks on the calculators' rims and hold them no more than 5 cm (2 inches) apart. [The user's guide suggests](https://literature.hpcalc.org/official/hp39g40g-ug-en.pdf) it for handing out problems and for turning in homework. A cable connects the 39G to a PC running HP's Connectivity Kit, or to an aplet disk drive, and the infrared also prints to HP's 82240B printer.

### HOME

As on the 38G, calculations happen in the HOME view, with algebraic entry and a history you can scroll. The d/dx key takes derivatives: in the Function aplet, define a function, then a second one as its derivative, and evaluate it.

## In Saturnus

Saturnus was tested with the ROM hpcalc.org offers for the 39G and 40G, which the app links to for download:

- The [saturnng](https://codeberg.org/gwh/saturnng) emulator, which Saturnus compares its screens with on the 48 and 49 models, does not run the 39G, and no real 39G has been compared yet. Its start, reset, memory clear and arithmetic are covered by Saturnus's tests.
- The speed is set as for the 49G, which has the same design; no real 39G has been timed for it. For long programs, switch to 2×, 4× or Max.
- Files can serve a folder to the 39G as the "disk drive" it sends aplets to and receives them from: on the calculator, APLET, then SEND or RECV, and "Disk drive…". Binary aplets, games among them, can also go over the calculator's wire transfer, "HP39/40 (Wire)". This works with the emulated 39G; with a real 39G on a cable (in Chrome or Edge on a computer) it has not been tried.
- The infrared port is not emulated, so beaming to another 39G or printing is not possible.
- There is no memory view for the 39G; it exists for the 48SX, 48GX and 49G.

The [command reference](commands.md#39g) lists the 39G's 209 functions with the keys that run them, and examples for 124 of them, run in Saturnus.

## Questions and answers

### Where do I get the HP 39G's ROM?

Saturnus does not include it: the ROM is HP's software. hpcalc.org offers [the ROM of the 39G and 40G for use with emulators](https://www.hpcalc.org/details/6739). In the browser, download the file, unzip it and drop it onto the app. The browser keeps it, and it never leaves your device. The desktop app and `saturnus rom fetch` on the command line download it for you after asking.

### Can I use the HP 39G online, in my browser?

Yes. [The app](../app/#/calculator/39g) runs in the browser on a computer or a phone, with nothing to install. You choose the ROM file once; the calculator then runs on your device, not on a server, and the app works offline after the first visit. On a phone you can add it to the home screen.

### Is there an HP 39G emulator for Mac, Windows and Linux?

Yes. The Saturnus desktop app runs the 39G and the other eleven models. It is free and open source, for Macs with Apple silicon and for Windows and Linux on Intel, AMD or ARM processors: [the downloads](../#install) are on Saturnus's site. For a Mac with an Intel processor there is no desktop app; use the app in the browser.

### Can I move aplets between Saturnus and a real HP 39G?

Between your computer and the emulated 39G, yes. Files, the app's second view, serves a folder as the "disk drive" the 39G sends aplets to and receives them from: on the calculator, APLET, then SEND or RECV, and "Disk drive…". With a real 39G on a cable it has not been tried yet. [Files in the app](transfer/files.md) has the steps.

### Does the emulated 39G keep my work?

Yes. The calculator keeps its memory when you close or reload the app, as a real one does when switched off. Save state keeps a second copy that Load state brings back, and a state saved to a file (`.sat`) opens in the browser, the desktop app and the command line.

### How close is it to a real HP 39G?

It runs the 39G's own ROM, so the aplets, menus and messages are the calculator's. No real 39G has been compared with it yet, and the speed is set as for the 49G, which has the same design. The infrared port is missing, so beaming to another 39G and printing are not possible. [How Saturnus is built](how-it-is-built.md) describes the method and the tests.
