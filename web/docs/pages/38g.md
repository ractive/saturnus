The HP 38G is a graphing calculator for secondary school maths and science that Hewlett-Packard introduced in April 1995. It runs on the hardware and much of the software of the HP 48G, but with algebraic entry and a simpler design built around aplets: small applications for one topic that a teacher can beam to the whole class.

![An HP 38G in Saturnus showing the square root of 2](../img/38g-640.webp)
The 38G as Saturnus draws it, with the square root of 2 in its HOME view.

Saturnus is a free emulator of the HP 38G. It runs the 38G's own ROM in your browser, as a desktop app for Mac, Windows and Linux, and on the command line.

## History

### Designed with teachers

HP set out to make a graphing calculator for students before calculus and their teachers. To help, it formed an Education Advisory Committee of eight teachers from high schools, community colleges and universities. The team met them every few months and kept in touch by email in between.

The teachers wanted students to explore mathematical ideas, but they also needed to keep them on track. At the first meeting the two sides compared this to a child's sandbox: toys to play with, inside a safe, closed space. So one of the main design goals was to encourage exploration by limiting choices. That led to the aplet, a small application that focuses on one problem. [Finseth's data sheet gives](https://www.finseth.com/hpdata/hp38g.php) the name as short for "Animated Personal LEarning Tool".

### Built on the 48G

The 38G reuses the [HP 48GX](48gx.md)'s platform. The [HP Journal article](https://www.hparchive.com/Journals/HPJ-1996-06.pdf) on it, from June 1996, lists what the two share: the same processor, 32 KB of RAM, 512 KB of ROM, the same 131 × 64 display, two-way infrared and an [RS-232](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RS-232) link. The input forms and choose boxes that made the 48G easier to learn run the 38G's views too. But the 38G is not an HP 48: it has no RPN and runs no HP 48 programs.

The case is the 48's, redesigned. A sliding plastic cover makes it tougher for younger students. Two keys were removed, so that the arrow keys stand out and the keyboard looks less complicated. HP's overhead projector display already worked with the 48G, but on the 38G the connector was changed so that every 38G can drive it, with no special calculator needed.

### Oregon and Singapore

The 38G was the first product of HP's Asia Pacific Personal Computer Division, after the charter for handheld products moved there from Corvallis, Oregon. The software team was split between Oregon and Singapore, more than 9,000 miles and eight time zones apart. [The HP Journal describes](https://www.hparchive.com/Journals/HPJ-1996-06.pdf) how they worked: "virtual team meetings" with headsets and a shared document on every screen, and a project web page that collected email discussions, plans and test documents. In 1995 that was new.

### Launch and price

[According to Finseth's data sheet](https://www.finseth.com/hpdata/hp38g.php), HP announced the 38G on 6 April 1995 at the 75th annual conference of the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Teachers_of_Mathematics) in Boston, with a special edition made for the event. [HP's virtual museum gives](https://web.archive.org/web/2008/http://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/museum/personalsystems/0026/index.html) its price as $79.95; [Finseth lists](https://www.finseth.com/hpdata/hp38g.php) $118. The code name was Elsie, after a cow in a food company's advertising. Among the 38G's firsts [the data sheet counts](https://www.finseth.com/hpdata/hp38g.php) the hard cover, the split screen, aplets, implied multiplication and the table view of equations.

A 38G+ was prototyped and cancelled in 1998. In 2000 the [HP 39G](39g.md) and, in Europe and other markets, the [HP 40G](40g.md) replaced the 38G.

## What makes it special

### Aplets and views

The design of aplets follows the "three views" of the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics: a problem can be seen as a graph, as symbols or as a table of numbers. Three keys open them: PLOT, SYMB and NUM. VIEWS offers more, such as a split screen with the plot beside its table. Each view has a setup screen, and a note view and a sketch view let you add text and drawings to a problem.

Six aplet types are built in: function, parametric, polar, sequence, statistics and solve. At first they are empty. You add the equations, settings, notes and sketches, and save the result under its own name. Each aplet keeps its own settings, so switching from one to another disturbs neither. LIB opens the library of aplets.

[James Donnelly's article in the same HP Journal starts](https://www.hparchive.com/Journals/HPJ-1996-06.pdf) with the function SIN(X²)/X: put it in the function aplet, zoom into the plot, and the aplet now holds that function, its scales and its modes. Send it to another 38G, and it opens there exactly as it was saved.

### A class with one setup

A teacher can build an aplet on the calculator, or on a computer, and beam it by infrared to every student's 38G. [HP's virtual museum describes](https://web.archive.org/web/2008/http://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/museum/personalsystems/0026/index.html) the point: at the start of a lesson, every calculator in the room is set up the same way. Students explore within the aplet on their own and can send their work back. A 38G can also save aplets to a computer over its serial cable, so teachers could share them online. On the PC, HP's Connectivity Kit for the 38G came with HPComm38, later [HPGComm](transfer/kermit-xmodem.md#hpcomm-and-hpgcomm), a Windows program by the author of Saturnus and Mitch Davis of HP.

### HOME, Ans and help for beginners

Calculations are done in the HOME view, which keeps a history you can scroll. Each result is stored in a variable named Ans. Start an entry with +, −, × or ÷ and the calculator puts Ans in front; press ENTER on an empty line and it repeats the last entry. [The HP Journal's example](https://www.hparchive.com/Journals/HPJ-1996-06.pdf) is balancing a chequebook.

To help beginners, a fraction format turns results into fractions, and the 38G reads a missing multiplication sign as implied multiplication. Most variables have a fixed type: X holds a real number, Z1 a complex number, M2 a matrix, with ten variables in each group.

### Plots

The 38G draws function, polar and parametric plots, sequences as stairstep and cobweb diagrams, and statistics as histograms, scatter plots and box-and-whisker plots. Tables can sit beside a plot, and tracing a curve shows its values.

### Hidden things

[Finseth's data sheet lists](https://www.finseth.com/hpdata/hp38g.php) some undocumented features: pressing ON and PLOT together copies the screen into a graphic variable, and the case cover has the number 6.0030000004 moulded into it.

## In Saturnus

Saturnus was tested with ROM A1.67, which the app links to for download:

- The [saturnng](https://codeberg.org/gwh/saturnng) emulator, which Saturnus compares its screens with on the 48 and 49 models, does not run the 38G. Where a result looked wrong, it was checked on a real 38G. ACOT is one: it ignores the angle mode, so ACOT(1) gives .785398163397 in degrees, where 45 is right. A real 38G does the same, so it is the ROM, and the command reference notes it.
- The speed is set as for the 48GX, which has the same chip; no real 38G has been timed for it. For long programs, switch to 2×, 4× or Max.
- Files can serve a folder to the 38G as the "disk drive" it sends aplets to and receives them from: on the calculator, LIB, then SEND or RECV, and "Disk drive". It works with the emulated 38G, and both ways with a real 38G on its 10-pin cable in the desktop app.
- The infrared port is not emulated, so beaming an aplet to another 38G is not possible.
- There is no memory view for the 38G; it exists for the 48SX, 48GX and 49G.

The [command reference](commands.md#38g) lists the 38G's 208 functions with the keys that run them, and examples for 121 of them, run in Saturnus.

## Questions and answers

### Where do I get the HP 38G's ROM?

Saturnus does not include it: the ROM is HP's software. hpcalc.org offers [ROM A1.67 for use with emulators](https://www.hpcalc.org/details/4775). In the browser, download the file, unzip it and drop it onto the app. The browser keeps it, and it never leaves your device. The desktop app and `saturnus rom fetch` on the command line download it for you after asking.

### Can I use the HP 38G online, in my browser?

Yes. [The app](../app/#/calculator/38g) runs in the browser on a computer or a phone, with nothing to install. You choose the ROM file once; the calculator then runs on your device, not on a server, and the app works offline after the first visit. On a phone you can add it to the home screen.

### Is there an HP 38G emulator for Mac, Windows and Linux?

Yes. The Saturnus desktop app runs the 38G and the other eleven models. It is free and open source, for Macs with Apple silicon and for Windows and Linux on Intel, AMD or ARM processors: [the downloads](../#install) are on Saturnus's site. For a Mac with an Intel processor there is no desktop app; use the app in the browser.

### Can I move aplets between Saturnus and a real HP 38G?

Yes. Files, the app's second view, serves a folder on your computer to the 38G as the "disk drive" it sends aplets to and receives them from: on the calculator, LIB, then SEND or RECV, and "Disk drive". This works with the emulated 38G, and with a real 38G on its 10-pin cable in the desktop app. [Files in the app](transfer/files.md) has the steps and [Cables](transfer/cables.md) the cable.

### Does the emulated 38G keep my work?

Yes. The calculator keeps its memory when you close or reload the app, as a real one does when switched off. Save state keeps a second copy that Load state brings back, and a state saved to a file (`.sat`) opens in the browser, the desktop app and the command line.

### How close is it to a real HP 38G?

It runs the 38G's own ROM, so the aplets, menus and messages are the calculator's. Results that looked wrong were checked on a real 38G. The speed is set as for the 48GX, which has the same chip; no real 38G has been timed. The infrared port is missing, so an aplet cannot be beamed to another 38G. [How Saturnus is built](how-it-is-built.md) describes the method and the tests.
